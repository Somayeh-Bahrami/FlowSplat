\paragraph{Geometry initialization.}
Each view of a rotational DSA sweep is acquired at a different time, so a
static reconstruction sees an inconsistent object. We therefore take the
vessel geometry from 4DRGS~\cite{liu2025fourdrgs}, which models static
Gaussian geometry with a time-dependent attenuation, and voxelize its
time-averaged model on the reconstruction grid. The centerline is the
longest geodesic path of the skeleton of the thresholded volume, and the
lumen radius $\hat R(s)$ at arc length $s$ is the distance at which the
cross-sectional profile falls to half of its central value, taken as the
median over 16 directions. This half-maximum criterion is independent of
the intensity scale, which matters because each segment is opacified for
a different fraction of the sweep. The half-maximum radius of the 4DRGS
volume is biased low, so we use $R(s) = k\,\hat R(s)$ with a single factor
$k = 1/\sqrt{\operatorname{median}_c\, \bar A_c/A_c}$, where
$\bar A_c/A_c$ is the ratio of estimated to true mean lumen area on
training case $c$ ($k = 1.086$ from 15 training cases; the validation and
test cases are not used). We place one Gaussian every 0.5\,mm along the
centerline, map it to its arc length $s_i$, align its major axis with the
local tangent, and set its cross-sectional scale to
$\sigma_i = R(s_i)/\sqrt{2}$, so that $A(s_i) = 2\pi\sigma_i^2$. Scales use
the bounded activation of 4DRGS~\cite{liu2025fourdrgs}.

On 20 validation cases with 60 views, the centerline covers the whole
vessel including the lesion in every case (lesion coverage 100\,\%,
vessel coverage at least 97\,\%). Two simpler initializations fail in
complementary ways: an FDK volume of the same views (Hann filter,
TIGRE~\cite{biguri2016tigre}) loses dim, early-filled segments (one case
misses its stenosis entirely) and mis-sizes the throat, and a static
R$^2$-Gaussian~\cite{zha2024r2gaussian} fitted to the dynamic views drifts
away from the vessel (radius error 0.20 to 0.42\,mm).
