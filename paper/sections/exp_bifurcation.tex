\paragraph{Bifurcations.}
We extend the transport model to a Y-shaped vessel: a parent segment and
two daughters, each daughter starting at the junction with the parent's
transit time, $\tau_i = \tau_J + \int_0^{s_i} A/Q_k\,\mathrm{d}s$, and one
flow per branch. Continuity is enforced as a hard constraint by
parameterizing $Q_p = Q_1 + Q_2$; Murray's law enters as a soft prior on the
flow split, $(\log Q_1/Q_2 - 3\log R_1/R_2)^2$, because the geometry is kept
fixed. The branches are extracted from the 4DRGS~\cite{liu2025fourdrgs}
volume without ground truth (longest skeleton path and its longest side
branch), and the inlet is chosen among the three free ends by the fit loss.
The bifurcation set contains 40 left anterior descending arteries with their
first diagonal from ImageCAS~\cite{zeng2023imagecas} with a Murray-consistent
flow split, one stenosis, and the bolus transport of each branch simulated
with the parent's outflow as inlet~\cite{thakur2026punch}; 24 geometries form
the test set.

\paragraph{Results.}
Table~\ref{tab:bifurcation} reports the flow split error, the relative error
of each daughter's share of the parent flow. With continuity, FlowSplat
recovers the split with a mean error of 3.8\,\% and the parent flow with
5.4\,\%; without it, the parent flow is fitted almost independently of the
daughters and both errors rise to 17 to 21\,\%. The Murray prior does not
help, even though the simulated split follows Murray's law: it relies on the
reconstructed daughter radii next to the junction, where the reconstruction
is least accurate. The automatic tree extraction failed on 4 of 24 test
vessels, all with a parent shorter than 25\,mm, which is not resolved as a
separate branch in the time-averaged reconstruction.
